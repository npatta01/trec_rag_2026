Capacity and retention explain the gap. GPT improves strict coverage by 0.040 over the controlled Qwen run, but seven of 42 candidate sentences are removed by the one-passage support gate. The original response had more answer capacity and broader evidence support, though it exceeded the competition word limit and retained two unsupported claims.

The selected runner therefore protects the format and faithfulness constraints, but it should not be treated as the final optimal architecture. A stronger citation-evidence selector or a bounded evidence-backed repair lane could recover supported claims without changing the generator comparison.

Recommendation

Deploy the selected lane, then improve evidence retention.

Use now

  • GPT-5.6 Sol as the fixed claim-preserving generator.
  • A facet-balanced frozen claim ledger targeting 900-1,000 candidate words.
  • Deterministic direct ClimbMix citations restored after generation.
  • Local Qwen support auditing with unsupported sentences excluded.
  • Hash-sealed organizer JSONL and lineage artifacts.

Next controlled changes

  1. Select up to three mutually consistent passages when one passage is insufficient.
  2. Measure final submitted-word retention, not only candidate-word compliance.
  3. Test a citation-only support repair that cannot alter claim semantics.
  4. Repeat over all development topics and use held-out topics for model selection.
  5. Replace the local-Qwen-only judge with a calibrated multi-judge audit.

Promotion rule: retain GPT-5.6 Sol while it leads strict coverage under identical evidence; change the generator only when a multi-topic controlled run improves strict and vital coverage without increasing unsupported submitted sentences.
