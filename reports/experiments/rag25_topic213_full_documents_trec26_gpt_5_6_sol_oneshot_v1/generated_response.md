# Topic 213: Korean War

## What triggered the Korean War?

The Korean War began on June 25, 1950, when the North Korean People’s Army launched a surprise invasion across the 38th parallel into South Korea and swiftly captured Seoul. [shard_05769_78488; shard_00214_42979; shard_01408_73258]

## How did Cold War strategy influence US actions?

As the first major test of containment, the war prompted the United States to resist communist expansion while avoiding a wider global conflict, and MacArthur’s proposal to expand hostilities against China intensified debate over those competing goals. [shard_00640_15425; shard_00839_47882; shard_00276_43322]

## What motivated US involvement in the Korean War?

Fear that communism would spread through Asia led the United States to defend South Korea after the invasion, heading a United Nations coalition whose military assistance was authorized by Security Council Resolution 83. [shard_00238_42441; shard_02139_77072; shard_02011_43116]

## How did the Korean War conclude?

After a military stalemate, fighting stopped under the July 27, 1953 armistice, which created a 2.5-mile-wide fortified DMZ near the 38th parallel but, because it was a truce rather than a peace treaty, left the conflict technically unresolved. [shard_01076_15403; shard_01259_11973; shard_00839_47882]

## How does the Korean War affect Korea

The war killed more than two million Koreans, and its armistice preserved Korea’s division without a formal peace treaty while creating the heavily fortified DMZ that still separates North and South. [shard_00627_41410; shard_00898_17145; shard_01259_11973]

## What motivated China involvement in te Korean War?

China entered to support North Korea against the US-led UN forces because advances toward the Yalu River threatened the collapse of its ally and the loss of North Korea as a protective buffer state. [shard_00627_41410; shard_02011_43116; shard_05252_10180]

## How did US presidents differ in their views on the Korean War?

Truman rejected MacArthur’s proposals to expand the war into China and use nuclear weapons because he sought to prevent a world war, whereas Eisenhower promised an early and honorable end, visited the Korean front, and concluded that negotiations offered the only way out.
